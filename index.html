About

A School-Wide Capability for Teaching Innovation

The Centre for Pedagogical Innovation (CPi) is a proposed school-wide capability at Saïd Business School, University of Oxford. It creates a coordinated setting for faculty-led development of AI-enabled, experiential, assessment-aware, and research-informed pedagogy across the programme portfolio.

The CPi serves faculty, researchers, students, and teaching staff across undergraduate, MBA, EMBA, specialist Masters, DPhil, Executive Education, and Oxford Saïd Online contexts, with the potential to inform practice more broadly across the University as the model develops. It provides a structured, low-risk environment to experiment with new teaching approaches, evaluate their effectiveness, and translate findings into publishable research and improved practice.

This is a collaborative initiative being developed with academic co-sponsors, Teaching and Learning expertise, Digital and Information colleagues, Education Technology contributors, programme-facing colleagues, and wider University collaborators.

Our Foundation

Protecting What an Oxford Education Stands For

Close dialogue, formative feedback, and the development of judgement under intellectual pressure are what distinguish an Oxford education. Oxford's tutorial model and debate tradition require students to defend and revise their thinking under genuine intellectual pressure; these are precisely the pedagogical mechanisms that matter most when AI is present in learning. The CPI uses AI and immersive technology where they protect and extend that work across the School, freeing faculty time for the human craft of teaching, not replacing it.

Research and Use Cases

What we are investigating

CPi pilots are designed to answer specific questions about teaching with AI and immersive technology. Each line of inquiry will be tested through selected pilots across appropriate programme contexts, with structured evaluation built in from the start.

Use Case 01

Does AI deepen case discussion, or flatten it?

Testing whether generative AI used as a discussion co-pilot (scenario branching, prompts, counter-arguments) increases the quality of human reasoning in the room, or substitutes for it. Measured through discussion analysis and student outcomes.

Use Case 02

What transfers from XR to real decision-making?

Investigating which kinds of managerial judgement (cross-cultural negotiation, crisis response, ethical reasoning) are genuinely strengthened by immersive simulation, and which gains fade outside the headset.

Use Case 03

Can students learn to challenge AI well?

Examining whether structured analytics labs build a durable capacity to interrogate AI outputs (assumptions, biases, hallucinations) as a core management competency, rather than passive reliance.

Use Case 04

Does AI feedback help students develop, or just reassure them?

Studying the effect of AI-mediated formative feedback on written work: whether it accelerates learning and revision quality, or whether it produces fluent-but-shallow improvement that masks underlying gaps.

Use Case 05

What does an Oxford classroom look like at distance?

Asking which elements of the Oxford teaching experience (presence, dialogue, formative challenge) genuinely survive transmission through mixed-reality and AI-mediated engagement tools, and which require physical co-presence.

Scholarship of Teaching and Learning

Underpinning all five use cases is a formal SoTL programme: a supported environment where new teaching methods are developed, rigorously evaluated, and translated into publishable scholarship. Faculty receive seed grants and join a CPi-convened working group dedicated to research on their own teaching practice. Current CPi-supported initiatives include The Full Picture project, exploring holistic approaches to learning assessment at Saïd Business School. As Oxford's Academic Career and Reward Framework introduces formal recognition for teaching excellence, the CPi gives Saïd Business School faculty the evidence base and development record to progress under the new pathway.

Active Research Partnership

The CPi is developing a cross-site comparative research programme on AI and pedagogy across diverse global contexts, with partner institutions in the Global South. The work is grounded in Fulbright-supported scholarship and formal collaboration across the wider University.

The CPi in Practice

A Proposed Visible Hub for Teaching Innovation

The CPi operates as a virtual centre now and is designed around a proposed visible physical hub at Saïd Business School. The Nest is proposed as CPi’s visible physical hub, subject to School approval and the COO/Estates-led space-planning process. CPi would provide the pedagogical and operational brief; final layout, access, and current-user arrangements would be determined through that process.

A Vision for Saïd Business School

The Nest as the Proposed CPi Hub

The CPi design brief proposes The Nest as a shared, high-value environment for pedagogical experimentation, faculty co-design, AI-enhanced teaching, immersive learning, visitor-facing activity, and scholarly research into learning itself. The final layout would be determined through COO/Estates-led design work and consultation with current users, including Skoll-linked, entrepreneurship, careers, and visitor-facing activity as advised.

A place to experiment

  • Faculty try live AI and XR tools in low-stakes pilot sessions before bringing them into a teaching context
  • Peer observation and structured feedback turn one-off ideas into shared practice
  • Co-design clinics give faculty hands-on time with CPi staff to shape their pilots and seed-grant proposals

A way to share

  • A resource library and SoTL archive keep findings, materials, and methods available to faculty across the School
  • The Teaching at Saïd channels carry CPi interviews, case write-ups, and Canvas-based development pathways
  • Self-referral and faculty interest registration give any colleague a clear route in

Reach into teaching

  • What is tested in the Hub travels into live MBA, EMBA, MSc, and Executive Education sessions
  • Faculty receive assessment-redesign support and follow-on coaching as pilots move into their courses
  • Each pilot is evaluated so that what works can scale beyond the originating faculty member

Our Community

Teaching at Saïd: the Community the CPi Serves

Teaching at Saïd is the living community initiative that the CPi grows from and feeds back into. Launched before the CPi's formal establishment, it is the brand under which a culture of teaching excellence is being built across the School: faculty interviews, professional development workshops, a termly newsletter, and the Teaching at Saïd Canvas course used by academics across the School.

The CPi gives Teaching at Saïd its institutional infrastructure, its research rigour, and its formal mandate. Teaching at Saïd gives the CPi its community, its warmth, and its proof of concept.

What the next phase should prove

This section gives leadership a simple way to judge whether a pilot is worth scaling, repeating, or publishing.

Pilot 01

Teaching support with AI

Use AI to reduce preparation and admin time, while keeping faculty in control of tone, judgment, and academic standards.

  • Measure time saved in planning and feedback
  • Capture faculty satisfaction and confidence
  • Review quality with a clear human-in-the-loop process
Pilot 02

Immersive classroom moments

Test whether XR or simulation tools improve engagement, discussion quality, and retention in selected cases or modules.

  • Compare engagement against a baseline session
  • Collect student reflections and feedback
  • Assess whether the format is reusable across cohorts
Pilot 03

From pilot to practice

Turn successful experiments into templates, guidance, and shareable teaching assets the School can use again.

  • Document what worked and what did not
  • Create repeatable resources for faculty teams
  • Identify candidates for publication or wider rollout
